How PMI removal works

If you put less than 20% down, you're likely paying private mortgage insurance every month, often $100 to $300. If your home has gone up in value, you may have enough equity to cancel it early.

Most lenders want your loan balance at 75% to 80% of the home's current value, depending on how long you've had the loan. A current appraisal is how you prove it.

A PMI removal appraisal helps you:

Give your lender what it needs. Most lenders require an appraisal to cancel PMI based on today's value.

Keep the savings. Every month without PMI is money back in your pocket.

Call your lender first

Many lenders, especially on loans sold to Fannie Mae or Freddie Mac, order the appraisal themselves. In that case you can't choose your own appraiser. Ask your lender how its PMI cancellation works before you order anything.

If your lender lets you choose, which is often the case with credit unions and community banks, that's where I come in.

How it works

  1. Call your lender. Ask what it requires and whether you can use your own appraiser. Get it in writing if you can.
  2. On-site inspection. The visit usually takes 30 to 60 minutes. I measure the home, document its condition, and research recent sales nearby.
  3. Report delivery. Your report is usually done within 3 to 5 business days. Send it to your lender with its cancellation request.

Common questions

How do I know if I have enough equity?

Divide your loan balance by your home's estimated value. If the result is around 0.75 to 0.80 or lower, you may qualify, depending on your lender and how long you've had the loan. Your lender will want an appraisal to confirm, not an online estimate.

What if the value comes in lower than I hoped?

It happens. You'll know where you stand and roughly how much more value you need before you qualify.

How much does a PMI removal appraisal cost?

$550 for most single-family homes, townhomes and condos. If your PMI is $150 a month, the appraisal pays for itself in about four months.
