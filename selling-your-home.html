Why pricing right matters

Price too high and your home sits while buyers wonder what's wrong with it. Price too low and you leave money on the table. Both are avoidable.

Online estimates can't see inside your home, and the data behind them isn't always right. I've seen online listings off by hundreds of square feet. An appraisal uses actual comparable sales, your home's real features and condition, and what's happening in your neighborhood right now.

A pre-listing appraisal helps you:

Know your value before you list. Not after the home has been sitting for weeks.

Set a price you can explain. You'll know which sales support it and why.

Selling on your own?

An appraisal gives you a solid, independent number to list at, instead of guessing from online estimates. It's just as useful if you're working with an agent and want a second, unbiased opinion before you set the price.

The fee is small next to the cost of mispricing a home. And when the buyer's lender orders its own appraisal, a recent appraisal in the right range helps you know what to expect.

How it works

  1. Free consult. We'll talk about your home, your timeline and your goals. I'll give you an exact quote and explain what to expect.
  2. On-site inspection. The visit usually takes 30 to 60 minutes. I document the home's features, condition, and any updates that add value.
  3. Report delivery. Your report is usually done within 3 to 5 business days: a clear, well-supported value you can list with confidence.

Common questions

Is this the same as the buyer's lender appraisal?

It's the same type of report, but it's yours. If the buyer's appraisal comes in low later, you'll have a solid reference point.

What if the value is lower than I expected?

Better to know before you list than after. You'll have time to adjust your price, take care of condition issues, or see which updates buyers in your area will actually pay for.

How long is an appraisal good for?

An appraisal reflects the market when it's done. In a fast-moving market, try to have it done within 90 days of listing.

How much does a pre-listing appraisal cost?

$550 for most single-family homes, townhomes and condos. Unique homes, acreage or multi-family may cost more. I'll quote it upfront.
